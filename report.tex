\section{Architecture Comparison: Bi-Encoder vs. Cross-Encoder}

In Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), retrieval is tasked with identifying the most relevant chunks from an indexed corpus for a given user query. Two fundamentally distinct neural architectures are used for this purpose: \textbf{Bi-Encoders} and \textbf{Cross-Encoders}.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=boxbg,colframe=primaryblue!70,title=\textbf{Mathematical Formulations: Bi-Encoder vs. Cross-Encoder}]
\small
\textbf{Bi-Encoder (Independent Dual-Tower Representation):}
\begin{align*}
    \vec{u} &= \text{Pool}(\text{Transformer}_{\theta}(q)) \in \mathbb{R}^d, \quad
    \vec{v} = \text{Pool}(\text{Transformer}_{\theta}(c)) \in \mathbb{R}^d \\
    S_{\text{Bi}}(q, c) &= \cos(\vec{u}, \vec{v}) = \frac{\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v}}{\|\vec{u}\|_2 \, \|\vec{v}\|_2}
\end{align*}
\textit{Property:} Query tokens and chunk tokens \textbf{never interact}. Representations are decoupled.

\vspace{0.4em}
\textbf{Cross-Encoder (Joint All-to-All Multi-Head Self-Attention):}
\begin{align*}
    X &= [\text{CLS}] \circ q \circ [\text{SEP}] \circ c \circ [\text{SEP}] \\
    H &= \text{TransformerLayers}(X) \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d} \\
    S_{\text{Cross}}(q, c) &= \mathbf{W}^T h_{\text{CLS}} + b \in (-\infty, +\infty)
\end{align*}
\textit{Property:} Every query token attends directly to every chunk token across all self-attention layers.
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Core Mechanical Differences}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Token Interaction vs. Information Bottleneck:}
    In a Bi-Encoder (\texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}), the document passage $c$ is compressed into a single vector $\vec{v} \in \mathbb{R}^{384}$ via mean pooling. Squeezing an entire 300-word paragraph containing general rules, conditional waivers, and monetary penalties into 384 numbers creates an informational bottleneck. At inference time, the query vector $\vec{u}$ only measures coarse geometric alignment with $\vec{v}$. Query tokens never attend directly to chunk tokens. In contrast, a Cross-Encoder (\texttt{ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2}) concatenates $q$ and $c$, allowing bidirectional self-attention $\text{softmax}(QK^T / \sqrt{d_k})V$ to directly correlate specific query terms (e.g., \textit{``client negotiations''}) with conditional clauses inside the passage (e.g., \textit{``within 12 hours of flight arrival''}).
    
    \item \textbf{Indexability and Precomputation:}
    Because the Bi-Encoder produces document vectors $\vec{v}_i$ independently of any query, all $N$ chunks in a corpus can be computed offline once and indexed into an Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) index (e.g., HNSW in FAISS). Query matching requires only a single vector dot product search scaling in sub-linear time $\mathcal{O}(d \cdot \log N)$. For a Cross-Encoder, however, the joint input $[\text{CLS}] \circ q \circ [\text{SEP}] \circ c$ requires both the query and chunk to be known simultaneously; \textbf{offline vector precomputation is fundamentally impossible}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Detailed Case Study 1: Exceptions \& Corner Cases (TEST\_01)}
\textbf{Query:} \textit{``Can staff fly in premium cabins for international client negotiations?''}\\
\textbf{Target Policy Truth:} Section 2.4 allows Business Class for continuous non-stop international flights exceeding 8 hours, or exceeding 6 hours if the traveler is scheduled to deliver a client presentation or attend executive contract negotiations within 12 hours of flight arrival, subject to VP and CFO approval.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=failbg,colframe=primaryblue!50,title=\textbf{Pipeline 1 (Baseline Bi-Encoder) --- Omitted Condition}]
\small
\textbf{Retrieved Top 3 Context:}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
    \item Rank \#1: Section 2.1 (\textit{Standard Cabin Class Mandate}) --- Cosine: 0.5428
    \item Rank \#2: Section 1.3 (\textit{Designated Corporate Travel Booking Engine}) --- Cosine: 0.4127
    \item Rank \#3: Section 2.4 (\textit{Long-Haul International Travel Exception Clause}) --- Cosine: 0.4039
\end{itemize}
\textbf{LLM Generation (Answer 1 from Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct):}
\begin{quote}
\textit{``Yes, staff can fly in premium cabins for international client negotiations. However, they need to obtain prior written concurrence from both the Division Vice President and the Chief Financial Officer at least 10 business days before ticketing.''}
\end{quote}
\textbf{Analysis:} Because Section 2.1 dominated the prompt, the model stated that approval is required but omitted the essential flight duration criteria (>8 hours, or >6 hours with client presentation within 12 hours).
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=passbg,colframe=primaryblue!50,title=\textbf{Pipeline 2 (Cross-Encoder Re-Ranking) --- Complete Compliance}]
\small
\textbf{Re-Ranked Top 3 Context:}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Rank \#1 (Promoted +2): Section 2.4 (\textit{Long-Haul Exception Clause})} --- Cross-Score: +0.4351
    \item Rank \#2: Section 2.1 (\textit{Standard Cabin Class Mandate}) --- Cross-Score: -5.7229
    \item Rank \#3: Section 1.2 (\textit{Mandatory Pre-Trip Travel Authorization}) --- Cross-Score: -7.9689
\end{itemize}
\textbf{LLM Generation (Answer 2 from Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct):}
\begin{quote}
\textit{``Yes, staff can fly in premium cabins for international client negotiations under the long-haul international travel exception clause. The policy allows this exception when traveling on continuous non-stop international flights with flight durations exceeding 8 hours or 6 hours if the traveler is scheduled to deliver a client presentation or attend executive contract negotiations within 12 hours of flight arrival. Employees need prior written concurrence from both the Division Vice President and the Chief Financial Officer no later than 10 business days before booking their tickets.''}
\end{quote}
\textbf{Analysis:} Re-ranking promoted Section 2.4 to the \#1 position, allowing the model to accurately articulate the exact flight duration thresholds.
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Detailed Case Study 2: Negation \& Constraints (TEST\_02)}
\textbf{Query:} \textit{``What items are not covered on expense claims for client business meals?''}\\
\textbf{Target Policy Truth:} Section 5.3 strictly limits alcohol to 1 drink per diner, prohibits solo alcohol, and bans nightclub bottle service, adult entertainment, tobacco, and gambling losses.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Baseline Retrieval Failure (Direct Hallucination):} The Bi-Encoder ranked Section 5.1 at \#1 (cosine 0.5936) and Section 3.2 (\textit{Prohibited Hotel Incidentals}) at \#2 (cosine 0.5562), ahead of Section 5.3 at \#3 (cosine 0.4615). Because Section 3.2 sat higher in the prompt, the LLM (\textbf{Answer 1}) hallucinated that hotel amenities (in-room video on-demand, mini-bar, laundry for trips <5 days, spa, gym passes) were the non-covered items for client business meals!
    \item \textbf{Re-Ranking Resolution:} The Cross-Encoder promoted Section 5.3 to \textbf{Rank \#2} (score +1.1171) ahead of Section 3.2 (score -1.5279). As a result, the LLM (\textbf{Answer 2}) correctly identified the 1-drink alcohol cap, adult entertainment / bottle service restrictions, and tobacco/gambling bans.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Latency Discussion}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Pipeline 1 (Dense Baseline):} Executes in \textbf{$\approx 9.5$\,ms} on CPU (query embedding takes $\sim 9.0$\,ms; cosine dot product takes $<0.5$\,ms).
    \item \textbf{Pipeline 2 (Two-Stage Re-Ranking):} Adds an average overhead of \textbf{$\approx 128.0$\,ms} to evaluate joint self-attention across the 10 candidate pairs ($\sim 12.8$\,ms per pair on CPU), totaling \textbf{$\approx 137.5$\,ms}.
    \item \textbf{Interactive SLA Feasibility:} In human-computer interaction, latencies below 100--200\,ms are generally responsive. While $\sim 138$\,ms on CPU is acceptable, deploying the Cross-Encoder on GPU with batching typically drops Stage 2 latency from $\sim 128$\,ms down to $\sim 15$--$25$\,ms, bringing end-to-end retrieval well under 35\,ms.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scaling Question: Why Not Run a Cross-Encoder Over the Whole Collection?}

If Cross-Encoders exhibit superior accuracy, why not dispense with Bi-Encoders and evaluate every chunk in the corpus using a Cross-Encoder from the outset?

\subsection{Quadratic Computational Complexity}
In a Cross-Encoder, the sequence length is $L = |q| + |c|$. Multi-head self-attention scales quadratically: $\mathcal{O}(L^2)$ operations per layer. Scoring a corpus of $N$ chunks requires $N$ independent forward passes through the transformer:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Measured CPU Scaling:} Based on our measured CPU runtime of $\tau \approx 12.8$\,ms per candidate pair, scoring a corpus of $N = 1,000,000$ chunks would require:
    \begin{equation}
        T_{\text{CPU}} = 1,000,000 \times 12.8\,\text{ms} = 12,800\,\text{seconds} \approx \mathbf{3.5\text{ hours per query!}}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{GPU Server Estimate:} Even assuming enterprise GPU hardware executing a forward pass in $\tau \approx 2.0$\,ms:
    \begin{equation}
        T_{\text{GPU}} = 1,000,000 \times 2.0\,\text{ms} = 2,000\,\text{seconds} \approx \mathbf{33.3\text{ minutes per query!}}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}
Under concurrent user queries, exhaustive Cross-Encoder scoring is computationally impossible.

\subsection{Impossibility of Offline Indexing}
In a Bi-Encoder, chunk vectors $\vec{v}_i = \text{Encoder}(c_i)$ are static and completely decoupled from the runtime query. This allows all $1,000,000$ chunks to be precomputed once offline and indexed into Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) graph structures (e.g., HNSW in FAISS), answering queries in \textbf{5 to 10 milliseconds} ($\mathcal{O}(d \cdot \log N)$).

In contrast, the Cross-Encoder score $S(q, c_i) = \mathcal{M}([q; c_i])$ requires both inputs simultaneously. Because the score cannot be factored into independent document vectors, \textbf{no precomputed vector index can ever be built}. Searching the corpus requires an exhaustive linear scan $\mathcal{O}(N)$.

\subsection{The Two-Stage Solution}
The two-stage pipeline reconciles scalability with precision:
\begin{equation}
    T_{\text{Two-Stage}} = \underbrace{T_{\text{Bi-Encoder}}(1,000,000 \rightarrow 10)}_{\sim 5-10\,\text{ms}} + \underbrace{T_{\text{Cross-Encoder}}(10\text{ candidates})}_{\sim 15-25\,\text{ms (GPU) / } 128\,\text{ms (CPU)}} \approx \mathbf{25-138\,\text{ms}}
\end{equation}
Stage 1 filters out 99.999\% of irrelevant documents in milliseconds, allowing Stage 2 to apply deep cross-attention exclusively to the top 10 candidates.
